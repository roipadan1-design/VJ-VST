# B1: Dance with projection, and live AV / light performance

Strand B1 for *Before It Disappears*. A curated library of 38 entries in 6 groups, a top-8 ranking,
and "What this means for our show".

## Method and honesty notes

- **Every source here is marked [search].** WebSearch was the only working tool. WebFetch was tried
  on two artist pages (frieder-weiss.de, openendedgroup.com) and both were blocked by the egress
  proxy (`EGRESS_BLOCKED`), so I stopped trying. No page was **[opened]**. Every claim comes from the
  search-result text returned for the URL cited, and every URL appeared in a tool result. Where I add
  something from general knowledge, it is marked *(inference)* or *(from memory, not verified here)*.
- The session's WebSearch budget ran out before I had searched everything I planned. The gaps are
  listed under "Leads not verified" at the end.
- Where a year or a credit did not appear in results, it says **unknown**.
- **No overlap with existing docs.** Kohlberger, Kurokawa, Ikeda, Kubelka, Prudence and Warnecke are in
  `RESEARCH-REPORT.md` §C1–C7. Motion-for-slow-music craft is in §B3. They are not repeated here.
  Robert Henke *Lumière* was only an "unverified lead" there (§C6/C8). It is verified below.
- "Movement" numbers refer to the 10-movement arc in `ART-DIRECTOR-BRIEF.md` §3: 1 Waiting, 2 The
  moment leaves, 3 Reaching / slowing / replaying, 4 The frame, 5 The pivot (recall→create), 6 The
  cloud, 7 The shadow, 8 The inversion, 9 Transformation / merging, 10 The mark.

**Constraints every entry was judged against:** rear projection onto the one screen behind the
dancers; generative only; no faces, eyes, skulls or masks; no figurative bodies that double the
dancers; one operator on Ableton and a small Korg; cue-locked; mostly-black frames with light at
the edges; monochrome; rare correlated events.

## Group A: Real-time generative projection *with* dancers

### A1. Klaus Obermaier with Ars Electronica Futurelab, *Apparition*
- **Year:** 2004, performed on since then. **Links [search]:** https://ars.electronica.art/futurelab/en/projects-apparition/ ;
  https://vimeo.com/58661899 ; https://www.isea-symposium-archives.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/2009_Barnett_The_Dancing_Body_As_A_Screen.pdf
- **Credits:** dancers Desirée Kongerød and Rob Tannion; Futurelab's Christopher Lindinger and Peter
  Brandl; motion tracking by Hirokazu Kato.
- **What it looks like:** projection lands both on the background and on the bodies. A camera extracts
  the dancer's outline, and the "kinetic space" around them expands, contracts, ripples and bends as
  they move. The bodies seem to "shift shape, dissolve, mutate". *(From memory, not verified here:
  mostly white line and grid structures on black.)*
- **Principle:** the projection is a "potential performing partner", not a prop. The body and the
  architecture are made of the same light.
- **Take:** the idea that the *field* bends in response, so the screen reads as space rather than as
  a picture. With no tracking, a slow warp of the background grid driven by audio energy gives some of
  this.
- **Avoid:** body-mapping. We rear-project, so we cannot light the bodies, and the owner does not
  want the screen to double them. The piece also depends on a tracking camera and a dedicated
  programmer, which one operator cannot run.
- **Serves movement 8, the inversion:** *"The memory carries us."* The field that holds the dancers
  up.

### A2. Chunky Move (Gideon Obarzanek) with Frieder Weiss, *Glow*
- **Year:** created in late 2005, premiered 2006, toured more than 40 cities between 2006 and 2012.
  **Links [search]:** https://chunkymove.com/works/glow/ ; https://cmarchive.net/list-of-works/glow ;
  https://brooklynrail.org/2008/03/dance/the-lady-and-the-light-chunky-moves-glow-at-the-kitchen/ ;
  https://www.realtime.org.au/glow-2006/
- **What it looks like:** a solo dancer on a white floor, lit by an overhead infrared tracking system.
  The graphics are "not pre-rendered video playback but … constantly generated by various algorithms
  responding to movement." A glowing "chalk outline" follows her. She "starts leaving her shadow
  behind on the floor … after a few seconds' delay, the shadows, smudging, creep to her."
- **Principle:** light exists only where the body has been. The **delayed trace** is the image.
- **Take:** the delayed, smudged trail, a residue that catches up seconds later. That is our line
  *"we remember the last time we remembered it"* made visible. On a rear screen we cannot trace the
  real body, but we can give *audio events* a trail. Each event leaves a mark that drifts and fades
  over about 3–8 s *(proposal)*.
- **Avoid:** the "mutant, grotesque creature" register, and floor projection from overhead (we have
  no floor surface). Do not attempt tracking.
- **Serves movement 2:** *"Movement disappears before we can hold it."* Also movement 10, *"Only a
  mark."*

### A3. Chunky Move with Frieder Weiss and Robin Fox, *Mortal Engine*
- **Year:** 2008, Sydney Festival. Won the 2008 Helpmann Award and an Ars Electronica Honorary Mention
  (Hybrid Arts) in 2009. **Links [search]:** https://cmarchive.net/list-of-works/mortal-engine ;
  https://www.artshub.com.au/news/reviews/chunky-move-dance-review-mortal-engine-177244-2296736/ ;
  https://www.stagewhispers.com.au/reviews/mortal-engine-chunky-move ; https://bachtrack.com/review-southbank-centre-chunky-move-mortal-engine
- **Credits:** Obarzanek (direction and choreography), Weiss (interactive system), Fox (laser and
  sound), Ben Frost (composer).
- **What it looks like:** a "severely raked white stage", six dancers "mostly kept in darkness" by the
  interactive projection system. Projections "graphically illuminat[e] and exten[d]" the bodies inside
  a "deep black envelop", with "a world of shadows" and laser in the later part.
- **Principle:** **darkness is the default state.** Light is spent only where the performer earns
  it. Video, light, sound and laser come from one system, so each night differs.
- **Take:** a darkness-first luminance budget, where the screen is off until the music or a cue asks
  for it. Take also the Ben Frost sound world (dark, bassy, sparse), which is close to our sparse
  sections.
- **Avoid:** the "malevolent amoeba" / soul-shadow horror register, which is too close to "hell" if
  used literally. Avoid dependence on tracking.
- **Serves movement 7, the shadow:** *"every memory becomes / the shadow of itself."*

### A4. Adrien M & Claire B, *Hakanaï*
- **Year:** 2013. **Links [search]:** https://www.artechouse.com/program/hakanai/ ;
  https://moco15.movementcomputing.org/performance-hakanai/index.html ;
  https://www.vice.com/en/article/dancer-bends-light-in-stunning-projection-mapped-performance/
- **What it looks like:** a cube of white tulle with one dancer inside and projections on the veils.
  An "ever-evolving graphic universe generated in real time" is played live by a second artist "like a
  'digital score'". *(From memory, not verified here: white dot grids and line meshes that bend, fall
  and flow like rain on black.)*
- **Principle:** the name. *Hakanaï* is Japanese for what is "temporary and fragile, evanescent and
  transient, set between dreams and reality." The image is performed by a human operator as an
  instrument, not played back.
- **Take:** the **live visual interpreter** model, which matches our one operator. Take the white
  particles and lines on black, the monochrome, and the fragility. Treat the scrim-like quality as a
  texture idea (fine grids that seem to have depth).
- **Avoid:** the 4-sided tulle box, since we have one flat rear screen. Avoid the "wow, it reacts to
  her hand" interactivity. The owner wants field and weather, not tricks.
- **Serves movement 6, the cloud:** *"Suspended between presence / and absence."*

### A5. Adrien M & Claire B, *Le mouvement de l'air*
- **Year:** 2015. **Links [search]:** https://www.am-cb.net/archives/le-mouvement-de-l-air ;
  https://vimeo.com/145201272 ; https://www.am-cb.net/docs/AMCB-AIR-Dossier.pdf ;
  https://www.lightzoomlumiere.fr/realisation/le-mouvement-de-lair-de-adrien-m-claire-b/
- **What it looks like:** three dancers among asymmetric tulle screens. The projections play "with the
  transparency of the screens to reveal or conceal certain choreographic phases occurring behind".
  The images are "generated and put into motion in real time by a digital interpreter in the control
  booth". The theme is making "visible the invisible of air movement", from "the gentlest and slowest"
  to "the most powerful". Built with Millumin v2 and the Anomes team.
- **Principle:** the screen is **weather**: air, not objects. Its density hides and reveals the
  bodies.
- **Take:** this is the nearest precedent to our brief of "screen = field / memory / weather". Take the
  range of air behaviours (still, drifting, gusting) as a per-section motion vocabulary, and density
  as the thing that reveals and conceals.
- **Avoid:** multiple tulles and flying rigs, which are out of scope. Avoid the prettiness of an
  illustration of wind.
- **Serves movement 6:** *"Memory is a cloud. / Always changing. / Always drifting."*

### A6. Mourad Merzouki (Cie Käfig) with Adrien M & Claire B, *Pixel*
- **Year:** premiered 15 November 2014, Maison des Arts de Créteil. **Links [search]:**
  https://vimeo.com/114767889 ; https://numeridanse.com/en/publication/pixel/ ;
  https://kafig.com/pixel-creation2014?lang=fr
- **What it looks like:** 11 hip-hop and circus dancers, "thousands of pixels" projected on floor and
  backdrop, evolving "in real time based on the movements of the dancers". Music by Armand Amar.
- **Principle:** a large-scale particle field as a stage for a group.
- **Take:** proof that dot fields scale to a full company and a full evening. Take the *density*
  control of a dot field as a dramaturgical lever.
- **Avoid:** its crowd-pleasing illusionism (floor perspective tricks) and its full, bright frames.
  They are the opposite of mostly black.
- **Serves movement 4, the frame:** *"It keeps the outline, / never the whole story."* This is
  inference: sparse dots describe a shape without filling it.

### A7. Hiroaki Umeda, *Holistic Strata*
- **Year:** 2011, Exposition EXIT, Maison des arts de Créteil. **Links [search]:**
  http://hiroakiumeda.com/ ; http://hiroakiumeda.com/project.html ;
  https://zkm.de/en/event/2011/11/hiroaki-umeda-while-going-to-a-condition-und-holistic-strata ;
  https://www.straight.com/arts/346576/hiroaki-umedas-haptic-and-holistic-strata-summon-sensory-overload
- **What it looks like:** "with the opening sound of a crash, his body is suddenly covered in a galaxy
  of tiny lights, and he moves against a projected milky way that becomes a blizzard of swirling
  particles both behind him and on the floor."
- **Principle:** Umeda "dissolves all constituting elements into the same information units (pixel)".
  Body, light and sound are treated as equivalent, and he aims to "transmit sensations, rather than
  messages".
- **Take:** the sudden **correlated event**: one sound, one total change of field. That is our "rare
  correlated events" rule. Take the milky way turning into a blizzard as a single-parameter build.
- **Avoid:** his sensory overload (strobe density, full-field white). The dancers must stay the
  figure, so our blizzard stays sparse and edge-weighted.
- **Serves movement 5, the pivot:** *"it no longer recalls. / It creates."*

### A8. Rhizomatiks (Daito Manabe, Motoi Ishibashi) × ELEVENPLAY (MIKIKO) × Kyle McDonald, *discrete figures*
- **Year:** premiered 19–20 April 2018, Gray Area, San Francisco. **Links [search]:**
  https://rhizomatiks.com/en/work/discrete-figures/ ; https://research.rhizomatiks.com/s/works/discrete_figures/en/ ;
  https://daito.ws/en/archive/discrete-figures/ ; https://grayarea.org/event/discretefigures-rhizomatiks-research-elevenplay-dance-technology-performance/
- **What it looks like:** five dancers with drones, AI and machine learning, and "virtual dancers" /
  "simulated bodies" generated from movement data. The piece is inspired by Alan Turing.
- **Principle:** machine-generated movement as a counterpart to human movement.
- **Take:** only the *data-as-trace* idea, where movement becomes a point cloud or lines. Our Mesh
  Body is in this family, and could serve movement 5 as the moment an image stops tracing and starts
  inventing.
- **Avoid:** almost everything else. The virtual dancers **double the dancers**, which is banned. It
  needs drones, a large technical crew and heavy compute, all beyond one operator and an Iris Xe.
- **Serves movement 5:** *"Beyond that, // it no longer recalls."* Used as a warning.

### A9. Alexander Whitley and Memo Akten, *Pattern Recognition*
- **Year:** 2016. **Links [search]:** https://www.memo.tv/works/pattern-recognition/ ;
  https://fjordreview.com/alexander-whitley-pattern-recognition ;
  https://dancexchange.org.uk/performance_archive/pattern-recognition/
- **What it looks like:** two dancers and eight robotic spotlights that track and respond. A "learning
  machine choreographer … controlling the light dancer". The score is by Scanner and Oliver Coates.
- **Principle:** light as a third dancer that learns, with themes of "perception, memory, learning,
  and yearning".
- **Take:** the idea that the image *learns and remembers*. Section by section, the screen could
  "keep" a residue of earlier sections (a persistence buffer that is never fully cleared) *(proposal)*.
- **Avoid:** moving heads and tracking. Those belong to the lighting designer, not to us.
- **Serves movement 9:** *"Maybe memory / is what transforms us."*

### A10. Troika Ranch (Dawn Stoppiello, Mark Coniglio), *loopdiver*
- **Year:** premiere year unknown; the PBS documentary aired in 2011. **Links [search]:**
  https://contactquarterly.com/cq/unbound/view/loopdiver ; https://www.livedesignonline.com/projection/troika-ranch-s-loopdriver ;
  https://www.kpbs.org/news/2011/oct/19/loopdiver-journey-dance/
- **What it looks like:** six performers in "looped choreography, music, interactive visuals and
  lighting". It began from watching rehabilitation patients' repetitive motions.
- **Principle:** the **loop as content**, and the effort of breaking it.
- **Take:** a literal model for movement 3. The image repeats a gesture, and each pass degrades it a
  little (generation loss). Coniglio built Isadora, so this shows a small team can run it.
- **Avoid:** looping *video of the dancers*, which is footage and doubles the bodies. Loop generative
  gestures instead.
- **Serves movement 3:** *"We slow it down. / We replay it."*

## Group B: Projection or scrim as a second layer (historic foundations)

### B1. Merce Cunningham with Paul Kaiser and Shelley Eshkar, *BIPED*
- **Year:** 1999. **Links [search]:** https://www.mercecunningham.org/the-work/choreography/biped/ ;
  https://openendedgroup.com/artworks/biped.html ;
  https://www.kennedy-center.org/education/resources-for-educators/classroom-resources/media-and-interactives/media/dance/merce-cunningham--biped/ ;
  https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/men-in-dance/ghost-notation/
- **What it looks like:** motion-captured phrases (70 phrases from two dancers) turned into
  "chalk-like sketches of human bodies" and "abstract apparitions". They were projected on a gauzy
  scrim in front of the stage, "floating in front of and above the dancers".
- **Principle:** the ghost-notation of movement, with motion data rendered as hand-drawn line.
- **Take:** the **hand-drawn line quality**, and the idea of a trace that notates without
  illustrating. Take the fact that the figures are *above* the dancers, not level with them.
- **Avoid:** recognisable human figures. Even as chalk sketches, they double the dancers. Keep only
  abstract strokes: lines that remember a movement's path, not its body.
- **Serves movement 4:** *"A memory lives inside a frame. / It keeps the outline."*

### B2. Trisha Brown with Robert Rauschenberg and Laurie Anderson, *Set and Reset*
- **Year:** 1983. **Links [search]:** https://trishabrowncompany.org/repertory/set-and-reset.html ;
  https://www.rauschenbergfoundation.org/art/lightboxes/trisha-brown-and-rauschenbergs-creative-alliance ;
  https://www.tate.org.uk/whats-on/tate-modern/robert-rauschenberg/trisha-brown-dance-company-set-and-reset-1983
- **What it looks like:** *Elastic Carrier (Shiner)* hangs overhead: a translucent sheer-white box
  flanked by pyramids. Black-and-white newsreel collages from five 16 mm projectors on three screens
  are "endlessly dissolving and refracting through the scrim's translucent layers". The costumes are
  silk-screened pale grey to black.
- **Principle:** the image as a **weather of the city** that runs independently of the dance while
  sharing its time. The title names the choreographic method: set a structure, then reset it.
- **Take:** independence. The screen does not illustrate; it runs on its own clock. Take the
  grey-to-black monochrome as a range.
- **Avoid:** archival footage, which is banned. Avoid frenetic cutting.
- **Serves movement 1:** *"Life keeps moving."* Also 3: *"We give it names."* (inference: a structure
  that is set and reset).

### B3. Lucinda Childs with Philip Glass and Sol LeWitt, *Dance*
- **Year:** 1979, commissioned by BAM. **Links [search]:** https://www.walkerart.org/reader/sol-lewitts-expanding-grid/ ;
  https://whitney.org/exhibitions/lucinda-childs ;
  https://www.artforum.com/features/dance-lucinda-childs-philip-glass-and-sol-lewitt-208982/
- **What it looks like:** a 35 mm black-and-white film of the same dancers, on a grid, projected on a
  scrim covering the front of the stage. The filmed and live dances are synchronised yet "seemed at
  times to be two different activities". The filmed dancers "appear to flit through the live
  performers".
- **Principle:** the replay superimposed on the live event: memory and present at once.
- **Take:** the **grid as a memory container**, and the idea that image and live dance share a tempo
  without sharing content.
- **Avoid:** filmed dancers, which is footage and doubles the bodies. Avoid a front scrim, since we
  rear-project.
- **Serves movement 3:** *"We replay it."* Also 9: *"where the moment ends, / and where we begin."*

### B4. William Forsythe with ZKM, *Improvisation Technologies*
- **Year:** CD-ROM 1999; the project began in 1994; online since 30 June 2023. **Links [search]:**
  https://zkm.de/en/media/videos/william-forsythe-improvisation-technologies-1999 ;
  http://www.medienkunstnetz.de/works/improvisation-technologies/ ;
  https://openendedgroup.com/writings/danceGeometry.html
- **What it looks like:** over 100 short video lectures in which "animated white lines" are drawn over
  Forsythe's movement. Lines, curves and planes hang in the air where a limb passed.
- **Principle:** in Forsythe's words, "movements leave visible traces in space."
- **Take:** this is the grammar of our fine plexus lines, justified choreographically. **One white
  line that persists after the gesture that made it** is the core image of "only a mark". Without
  tracking, we can generate strokes whose *curvature* echoes dance phrasing: slow arcs, not
  jitter.
- **Avoid:** diagrammatic overlays on bodies.
- **Serves movement 10:** *"Only a mark. // The quiet mark // left // by everything // that ever //
  moved us."*

### B5. William Forsythe, Maria Palazzi and Norah Zuniga Shaw, *Synchronous Objects for One Flat Thing, reproduced*
- **Year:** launched 1 April 2009 (Ohio State, ACCAD). **Links [search]:** https://synchronousobjects.osu.edu/ ;
  https://accad.osu.edu/research-gallery/synchronous-objects
- **What it looks like:** screen-based visualisations (3D animation, annotation, interactive graphics)
  of the choreography's counterpoint and cues.
- **Principle:** the dance's hidden structure, turned into objects.
- **Take:** the idea of *cue lines*, which are visible connections between events. A plexus line that
  briefly links two correlated events (a sound and a light flicker) and then stays as residue.
- **Avoid:** the look of an infographic.
- **Serves movement 3:** *"We give it names."*

## Group C: Light-first dance scenography

### C1. Wayne McGregor with Random International, *FAR*
- **Year:** 2010. **Links [search]:** https://waynemcgregor.com/productions/far ;
  https://vimeo.com/23256430 ; https://www.theartsdesk.com/dance/far-wayne-mcgregorrandom-dance-sadlers-wells-theatre ;
  https://nac-cna.ca/en/stories/story/the-uks-wayne-mcgregor-random-dance-dazzles-with-stunning-visuals-and-extra
- **What it looks like:** a suspended "computerized pin board of 3,200 LED lights". Its "elegant
  mutations suggest star-fields and neural pathways", while separate lighting "emphasizes the dancers'
  bodies". The score is by Ben Frost; the theme is Diderot's *Encyclopédie* and the Enlightenment.
- **Principle:** **an abstract dot field behind or above the dancers**, low resolution, with the
  bodies lit by a separate rig. Field and figure never mix.
- **Take:** almost everything. A coarse dot matrix on black (a few thousand points) that reads as
  "star-field / neural pathways" is exactly a memory field. The low resolution makes it read as
  *light* rather than *picture*. Split the roles: the lighting designer lights the bodies, and we own
  only the field.
- **Avoid:** strobing the whole panel at the audience. Ration any full-panel flash.
- **Serves movement 1:** *"They wait. // Quietly."* The dormant field. Also 6.

### C2. Wayne McGregor, *Atomos* (a cautionary reference)
- **Year:** premiered 9 October 2013, Sadler's Wells, 65 minutes. **Links [search]:**
  https://waynemcgregor.com/productions/atomos ; https://artsfuse.org/170328/dance-review-atomos-way-overagitated/ ;
  https://criticaldance.org/wayne-mcgregor%E2%94%82random-dance-atomos/
- **Credits:** lighting Lucy Carter, film Ravi Deepres, live score A Winged Victory for the Sullen,
  costumes Studio XO.
- **What it looks like:** ten dancers under suspended screens showing film, with a 3D section viewed
  through glasses. A reviewer saw "floating squares, green words, and a red ball". The screens sat
  "too far above the stage", so "looking at them meant missing the more interesting live action".
- **Principle, as reviewed:** an overstuffed layering of media.
- **Take:** the music. A Winged Victory for the Sullen is ambient and neo-classical, the same world as
  our sparse sections. Their pairing shows that slow drones can carry a full-length dance.
- **Avoid:** **this is the key anti-pattern.** Image and dancers must sit in *one* visual field, so the
  audience never has to choose between them. Avoid readable words and literal objects on screen.
- **Serves:** none directly. It is a warning for movements 3–4 (the risk of *"We give it names"*
  turning into captions).

### C3. Wayne McGregor, *Autobiography*
- **Year:** premiered 4 October 2017, Sadler's Wells. **Links [search]:** https://waynemcgregor.com/productions/autobiography ;
  https://www.wallpaper.com/art/wayne-mcgregor-autobiography-v95-v96-sadlers-wells-london
- **Credits:** set Ben Cullen Williams, lighting Lucy Carter, music Jlin, algorithm Nick Rothwell.
- **What it looks like:** 23 short sections, one per chromosome pair. An algorithm based on McGregor's
  genome picks and orders the sections each night. The body is treated as an archive.
- **Principle:** memory as a **re-ordering archive**, so each performance is one "recall".
- **Take:** a dramaturgical model for reconsolidation. The same material is recalled in a different
  order each time. For us this could be a seeded variation per show: the same cues, with a different
  seed for noise and particle placement, so each night's "memory" is rewritten *(proposal)*.
- **Avoid:** running order by algorithm. Our show is cue-locked.
- **Serves movement 4:** *"The story is rewritten / every time it is remembered."*

### C4. Wayne McGregor, *Deepstaria*
- **Year:** premiered June 2024, Montpellier Danse. **Links [search]:** https://waynemcgregor.com/productions/deepstaria ;
  https://www.liftedleg.com/2025/03/company-wayne-mcgregor-deepstaria-london-review ;
  https://www.westk.hk/en/events/waynemcgregor_deepstaria
- **Credits:** Ben Cullen Williams; lighting Theresa Baumgartner; sound Nicolas Becker and LEXX.
- **What it looks like:** performed in a "light-absorbing Vantablack® space" with "extensive use of
  haze" to reveal laser and light effects. It is inspired by the deepstaria jellyfish.
- **Principle:** **maximum black**, and light that exists only in the air.
- **Take:** confirmation that a current major company chooses near-total black and haze. Our lifted
  projector black is the enemy (see the notes on black level at the end).
- **Avoid:** lasers and strobe density. They are not ours, and there are photosensitivity concerns.
- **Serves movement 1:** *"Like silence."*

### C5. Russell Maliphant and Michael Hulls, *AfterLight (Part One)*
- **Year:** 2009, a solo for Daniel Proietto. **Links [search]:** https://www.michaelhulls.com/afterlight ;
  https://www.britishtheatreguide.info/reviews/afterlight-rev
- **What it looks like:** "the dancer begins in the dark, turning imperceptibly against the direction
  of the revolving particles of light projected overhead" (projection by Jan Urbanowski). It was
  inspired by Nijinsky's photographs and his geometric drawings.
- **Principle:** **counter-rotation.** The field and the body turn in opposite directions, so each makes
  the other visible.
- **Take:** a direct compositional rule. When dancers spin or travel, the field drifts the other way
  at a slow, constant angular speed. Our Mesh Body already counter-rotates its layers. Take also Hulls's
  darkness-first approach, where light *is* the set.
- **Avoid:** the literal swirl of a galaxy, which is a cliché.
- **Serves movement 9:** *"where the moment ends, // and where we begin."*

### C6. Akram Khan with Anish Kapoor, *Kaash*
- **Year:** 2002, revived in 2014. **Links [search]:** https://www.akramkhancompany.net/productions/kaash-2002/ ;
  https://criticaldance.org/contemporary-cataclysms-akram-khan-company-in-kaash/ ;
  https://www.seeingdance.com/perfectly-formed-akram-khans-kaash/
- **What it looks like:** a huge black rectangle on the backdrop, "a geometrical black hole around which
  changing colours pulsate". It "sometimes glowed with primary colors and sometimes seemed menacing as
  it sucked the light from the rest of the stage into its black center."
- **Principle:** **the void is the image. Light lives only at its edges.** This is a painter's version
  of the owner's own rule.
- **Take:** a near-literal template for our field. Keep a central dark mass behind the dancers, with a
  slow luminous halo at its perimeter. It also answers movement 4 ("a frame") and gives a way to fade
  the halo away without ever going to a flat black screen.
- **Avoid:** primary colours. Stay monochrome.
- **Serves movement 4:** *"The edges begin to soften."*

### C7. Crystal Pite, *Flight Pattern* and *Light of Passage*
- **Year:** *Flight Pattern* premiered 16 March 2017 at the Royal Opera House; expanded into *Light of
  Passage* in 2022. **Links [search]:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_Pattern ;
  https://www.gramilano.com/2022/10/review-crystal-pite-light-of-passage/ ;

## What this means for our show

1. **Darkness is the default; light is spent.** Following *Mortal Engine*, *Deepstaria* and Tim
   Hecker, every section starts from black, and the screen earns each photon. Proposed **average
   picture level (APL) budget**: waiting and silence sections ≤3–5%; dance sections ≤12–15%; peaks
   (the pivot, correlated events) ≤35% for no more than 2 s; the finale ramps from 8% to about 25% over
   5 minutes. These are proposals to verify with the lighting designer and the projector in the venue
   (strand C).
2. **Keep a dark band behind the torsos.** Rear projection puts the dancers in front of the light
   source. A bright field behind them turns them into silhouettes and fights their front and side
   light. Proposed layout: keep roughly the **25–65% frame-height band** (behind torsos and heads, from
   the audience's eye line) below about 5% luminance in dance sections. Put light at the **top 25%, the
   side 15% margins and the bottom edge** (the *Kaash* rule and the owner's edge rule). *(Inference;
   calibrate with real sightlines.)*
3. **Use silhouette only as a deliberate event.** Reserve a bright field behind the dancers for
   movement 7, "the shadow", so they *become* shadows against memory. Hold it for one phrase (8–16 s),
   and cue it; never let it happen by accident.
4. **Real shadows need the lighting designer, not us.** Rear projection means the dancers cast no
   shadows on our image. If movement 7 needs *real* shadows, the lighting designer can throw a low
   frontal source onto the screen while we drop the projection to near-black in that zone. It is a
   joint cue (a proposal to discuss).
5. **Field and figure never share content.** No figurative bodies, no outlines of dancers, no
   motion-captured figures (the lesson of *BIPED*, *Lovers* and *discrete figures*). The screen may
   *echo* phrasing (curvature, tempo, counter-rotation as in *AfterLight*) but never shape.
6. **One visual field.** The image sits on the same plane as the dancers, directly behind them, and
   never pulls the eye to a separate rectangle (the *Atomos* lesson). Avoid a strong central vanishing
   point (tunnels, corridors) while dancers are on stage.
7. **Coupled versus uncoupled sounds** (Henke). For each section, list the few sounds that *own* a
   visual gesture, and let everything else, especially steady percussion, drive only slow *clocks*
   (speed of drift, grain), never position or brightness. See RESEARCH-REPORT §B3.
8. **Rare correlated events.** At most about 1 event per 16–32 bars in sparse sections, and 1 per 8
   bars in percussive ones (Umeda's single "crash that changes everything"). Every event is authored
   as an Ableton cue or a clip-launched OSC message, never left to threshold detection alone.
9. **Every event leaves a residue** (*Glow*, Forsythe). An event draws a line or a dot cluster that
   stays and decays over 3–8 s. Across the whole show, a low-level persistence buffer keeps traces of
   earlier sections at 1–3% opacity, so by the finale the screen literally *carries* the evening
   (movement 8). This costs one extra full-screen buffer, M on the Iris Xe *(estimate)*.
10. **Weather, not objects** (*Le mouvement de l'air*, Janssens, Langheinrich). Each section's identity
    is a *kind of air*: still dots, drift, gust, noise-wave. Palette drift is a single-hue temperature
    shift over minutes, not colour changes.
11. **Seed the rewrite** (*Autobiography*). The same cues play every night with a different noise seed
    per show, so the memory is "rewritten every time it is remembered", while it stays repeatable
    enough to restore from a snapshot. Store the seed in the snapshot for recovery.
12. **Slowness under speed** (*Crowd*). In percussive sections, the image may stay slow while the music
    is fast. Map energy to drift speed with a slew of 1–4 beats. No cut-per-kick.
13. **Haze: do not expect beams.** With rear projection, beams are behind the screen, so McCall-style
    cones will not appear to the audience. Stage haze in front of the screen will pick up glow from the
    screen surface and *lift the blacks around the dancers*. Agree a haze level with the lighting
    designer and test the minimum-APL states in haze *(inference; verify in tech)*.
14. **No tracking in v1.** *Apparition*, *Glow* and *Mortal Engine* all needed an infrared camera and a
    dedicated programmer. For one operator on a small controller, a tracking system is an extra point
    of failure. Get the *feeling* of response from audio and cues instead.
15. **Finale as one line completing** (McCall, Pite). For "Because", one slow structure over about 5
    minutes, for example a Halo Ring that closes as the stacked voices build. Brightness rises slowly
    from about 8% to about 25% APL, and the last frame keeps a **mark** (the residue buffer from point
    9), not black.
